## Real2Real energy modeling and quantum sampling

This is our mechanism. We call it Real2Real energy modeling and quantum sampling for probabilistically global optimality. [seg 2548–2552]

Before I introduce our method, I would like to let you know how a traditional generative model works. Usually, we have the multimodal input, and we encode it into the latent space. After several layers, for example a transformer, we can transform the input latent space into the output latent space, the latent representation. And we usually do sampling after that, and we decode the vectors and spit them out. [seg 2553–2558]

But what's the problem with this paradigm? We can imagine if it is a Gaussian distribution — we all know what it is, right? So if a robot wants to get out of this room, and there are multiple doors, and we use a Gaussian model to fit the data — for example, we have data of a robot going out of the left door and the right door — if we train the robot to fit a Gaussian distribution with this data, the highest probability says the robot should go into the wall between the two doors instead of going into either one. So that's the problem. It is what we call incomplete multimodality representation, as well as the mode suppression problem. [seg 2559–2569]

Of course, there are ways to alleviate this problem. We can use multiple Gaussians, or a mixture of Gaussians, to solve this problem. However, there are still lots of artifacts in it, because how many Gaussians we are going to spit out is a number we need to predefine beforehand. Is there a distribution that allows us to fit any kind of complex multimodal distribution? And the answer is yes. [seg 2570–2577]

So this is the distribution we use. It is a Boltzmann energy landscape distribution. We can even mathematically prove that in the extreme case, if we sample a lot of data from the real world, this distribution converges to the distribution of our real physical world. So by leveraging this distribution — and the definition of this energy distribution is that the peak or the 〔unintelligible〕 [seg 2578–2585]

So we reduce the problem. Maybe it is 2 to the power of 1,000, and basically no GPU or CPU or any kind of classical computational device can figure out the answer in 1 to 3 milliseconds〔?〕. So this problem becomes tractable. [seg 2586–2587]

And it improves a lot of things, right — it addresses a lot of challenges. First, our Boltzmann energy landscape can be anchored to the distribution of our real physical world by the samples we collect from the real world. And in the extreme case, this distribution can be basically the same as our real-world distribution. And we leverage quantum sampling to solve the intractable problem. And for the multimodality problem: as I already presented, the Boltzmann energy landscape is able to capture any kind of multimodal distribution, probabilistically, right? So we can capture a lot of different modes in real problems, and we further present a quantum RSI, or quantum self-improvement, process with this method. [seg 2588–2597]

## Quantum RSI

We can use only a few — for example, several tens of real data points or real samples — to start our recursive process, and it anchors an initial energy landscape. And afterwards, we can use our quantum sampling to sample the global optimum from this initial landscape. The result of the sampling process is then executed with a real robot, and we repeat this process. [seg 2598–2603]

The interesting thing happens in how the landscape is updated after it has a new sample. Because, as I mentioned just now, in the extreme case, if we collect many more real robot execution samples, our energy landscape will finally converge to the distribution of our real physical world. So if we have another new optimal sample at this moment: if it is already the ground-truth global optimum, the process stops. However, if it is not, the next global minimum of the landscape appears immediately after the landscape is updated. So we have a mathematical proof that we get closer to the global optimum in our physical distribution. So this is a very efficient process. And this process can be put into the entire improvement process of the robot and our physical AI models. [seg 2604–2617]
