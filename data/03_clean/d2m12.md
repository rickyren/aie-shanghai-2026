## Why action matters

But why is action very important? Because if we take a deeper look — 10 years ago I needed to explain the concept of agent to our investors, to distinguish why it's not a chatbot. You develop a conversation; why do you call it an agent? So the key for us to understand agent is to understand the capability of taking actions. And this is not a new concept. Philosophically, it's a first-century〔?〕 concept — the concept of agent comes from Latin, by the way: someone taking actions. [seg 2268–2278]

But what are actions? We use computers; they perform a lot of operations. What's the difference between operation and action? A goal plus operation equals action. Who gives the goal to the system? It's the human being — it's intent, by the way. And what is a goal? A goal is the desired state. That means to take action is to transit the current state to another, desired state — to make this transition. So that's how we evaluate the effectiveness of actions for agents. If you can take action effectively, that means your agent can create an impact in the environment, that is, change the state of the environment. That sounds easy, as long as you own the environment. What if you want to take an action to change the state in other people's environment? Will other people allow you to do so? Think about why Amazon banned Muse recently. So that's the layer, I think, that is missing. [seg 2279–2298]

So my startup didn't really work out, and we used the framework to pivot to enterprise solutions. This is how I built the first AI concierge for Visa — the Visa Concierge, back in 2017. They had existing APIs that solved the problem of fulfillment, and we provided the language capability and the domain-specific logic approximation. That was how I pivoted from personal agent to enterprise agent. In the past eight years I built a lot of enterprise agents — BMW, Porsche, Intel, etc. We indeed called them agents, because even though the foundation tech was NLP, it was natural language to API. Not chatbot, not knowledge graph. [seg 2299–2310]

The thing that I learned from doing eight years of enterprise agents, especially when dealing with European companies, is the concept of sovereignty. And that is the action problem, the environment problem. We built an IPA, for example, for BMW — Intelligent Personal Assistant. It's in the car, and it belonged to one department. There's another, financial-service company under BMW, and there's the maintenance department as another department in this organization. They built different agents using different vendors, different frameworks or harnesses. What if a customer makes a request like "check my loan contract for my car" in front of the car〔?〕? They couldn't do this, because those agents were separated within one organization, even under the same brand, and on different frameworks as well. So that problem we solved in 2019 within one organization: we allowed different departmental agents to communicate with each other, framework-agnostic. But that was an enterprise solution. [seg 2311–2326]

And to me, it was too early. We charged them a lot of money, so we knew there were not going to be so many agents, because training one agent could only be afforded by MNCs back then, and they could have a better ROI to leverage the result, right? But then LLMs came out. I spend half of my time in San Francisco, half of my time in Shanghai. Everybody's doing agents now — 10 years later, using LLMs. And we noticed a lot of agents. This year, personal agent became a thing, right? [seg 2327–2338]

If we look at the landscape of what we have right now, we have these personal agents that can probably do a lot of things. And we figured there's a duality of service and client in terms of agents. That means one agent can probably provide service for you while at the same time consuming other services from other agents. That kind of duality probably gives us a better perception of the future of the agent economy. How are we going to connect them together? If we put it as "connectivity," that's a very big and vague word. How are we going to make it work, right? From the network layer to the actual commerce layer or business layer. How are we going to incentivize those agent developers to benefit from using any kind of protocol, right? And there's another landscape that we don't really recommend developers go into: if you want to build something that can fulfill everything, that falls into the category where nobody owns all the environments, right? [seg 2339–2356]

So this is how we see the future could be, or should be. This is what frontier model companies might think about for their monetization: you talk to the super personal agent — Codex, Claude Code or ChatGPT, or Dot〔?〕, for example — it collects your intent and dispatches it to different services. This is not new. If you guys experienced the digital assistant era 10 years ago, this already happened. It turned out not many brands wanted to join this structure of ecosystem, because they don't want to be white-labeled fulfillment. They don't want to be reduced to an API. They want to still hold the direct communication between their service and their customers. And how do they do that? [seg 2357–2366]

The problem of sovereignty is that it creates isolation, right? If everyone wants to own something, how are you going to do the exchange? You need to import and export something. You need a way to trade in a fair way, not in a zero-sum game. That was the experience that happened already 10 years ago. Another issue is the agent–principal problem, which means: if you guys tried your personal agent — Muse, Clawdbot〔?〕, Anthropic stuff — whose best interest is this personal agent serving? You, or the platform? How do you ensure that your personal agent won't give you recommendations that are not necessarily in your best interest, but rather ones where they could take a commission from the service providers, like what ad exchanges have been doing for years, right? [seg 2367–2378]

So sovereign agent is something that we think could be the foundation layer in the future, to ensure individuality and individual sovereignty. And that's from the demand side. From the fulfillment side: do you allow other people or other agents to touch your environment? Or would you rather set up an interface, so other human customers and their agents can interact through this interface with your backend system or your fulfillment layer? And that, I think, will in the near future be represented in the form of agents. So we will probably face two types of future, right? One is comparatively centralized. Centralization itself is okay, as long as you can create a fair game for all the stakeholders in this ecosystem. But if you choose a rather decentralized ecosystem, then we need to have a way, a layer, to allow those isolated or decentralized dots to be connected, and to communicate with each other in a fair way and build commerce. [seg 2379–2390]

At least this is how human beings have been — we built the entire human civilization on commerce. Each individual is sovereign, and we use language to communicate with each other. We developed all kinds of mid-layers, all kinds of marketplaces to exchange stuff. So that's why we think we are coming to the next phase of the agentic era. [seg 2391–2397]

## Agent is the new web; the network is the AI

The first part we call "agent is the new web." It's not an app. It's the new web, because the web is permissionless. It's open, right? That's how OpenClaw and other harnesses open up to developers. If you can quickly adopt it, the way you adopted HTML back then — this is how we compare agents to webs. And the next step will be "the network is the AI." Anybody heard of "the network is the computer"? Not many. That was TCP/IP, right? Basically, it promised a future which is the current reality: you do not need to own everything. If you have a device in front of you, and your device is connected to other people's devices through this network, you can visit the content on other people's devices rather than owning the storage and the compute on device. That is how the network works. [seg 2398–2412]

And we think in the future we could similarly do the same thing. That's why we call it "the network is the AI." If you have your personal agent, or your service or brand agent, you have your fulfillment issue — a boundary, a scope of the capabilities that you can deliver to yourself or your customers. What if the intent or requirement from your customer falls out of that scope? Can you do some trade or exchange? That's the issue we think will create a whole new layer upon applications: to build marketplaces — different kinds of marketplaces, not just one, not just one Google for the discoverability of all the webs, but different types of exchanges, trade places, marketplaces, except their users are agents. So this is a new interoperation layer that we think can be built upon a free network. If you're a company, a big organization, there's a governance layer, and you can continue using different types of models, different types of harnesses, even allow your employees to use different coding agents, as long as you can provide a way for them to access the permissions and the accessibilities while keeping their sovereignty — keeping individuality, choosing their preferred setups. [seg 2413–2425]

And if it's cross-organization, this is what we have done previously, but in a private way. You can share what we call intent exchange: you can share the intent that you cannot fulfill, and you can broadcast it to the other B2B partners you have already established cooperation with — not in the traditional B2B style, but in agent-to-agent style. And this is how we think B2B commerce, like procurement and sourcing, can have a much better procurement process. That's one example. For individuals, you can connect with each other, be friends — let your personal agents be friends. And this is not something in the far future. This is today; this is what's already happening. [seg 2426–2436]

So this is the future I encourage. If you're an agent builder, if you're an organization, or if you're not a builder but an individual who owns several personal agents, you can have a try. So probably when you guys are connected with each other, there will be a new growth of users that's building the foundation of a new layer of economy — not just human users, but all of our agents.
