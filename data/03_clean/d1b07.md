Hi everyone, my name is Roman. I work at Agoda, Booking Holdings, as an engineering manager. [seg 886–887]

Here's the challenge: our one big organization produces over 100,000 MRs per year. That is a huge amount of developer time, from my point of view. How can we reuse it? [seg 888–890]

Today I want to talk about two questions: how can we benchmark our context, and how can we improve our one-shots? In our team we came up with one simple idea: let's take our merged MRs, replay them, and learn something from them. [seg 891–892]

Based on it, we built our framework, which contains three steps. First, we prepare the task: we get our ticket and GitLab description and generate a prompt. Second, we ask the agent to replay. And the last step is that we evaluate our results against the golden tests that we already have in the merged MR. [seg 893–896]

## Benchmarking our context

Now my story is about one production service. Our team heavily invested in the context for this service: we have everything, we have agent and skills, architecture files. Let's call our service A. We decided to benchmark our context. Our first test: we take 10 MRs and do an A/B test. On the A side we remove all the context, and on the B side we keep it as it is. [seg 897–898]

The result was surprising: the solution rate was the same, but the cost was 8% higher with our context. So our context helps, but how can we optimize the cost? [seg 899]

Next we decided to optimize the cost, so we chose a context compaction tool. We took a popular GitHub project, Headroom, and ran the second A/B test. Cost per task dropped by 50%, but the pass rate decreased by 30% as well, so our agent became 30% dumber. Is it really worth it? Our team decided no, let's continue to search. [seg 900–901]

Next test, we took another tool, Graphify, which is AST-based. We ran our test and this time we finally saw a cost reduction: 24% lower for our test. Great results. Let's roll it out to the other services. But before that, let's run it against another repository. We ran the test against another repository, and this time we got completely opposite results: 30% higher cost. [seg 902–906]

After this we came to one single conclusion: we should focus on our own benchmark and our own repositories. There are too many changing factors, models, tools, repository size, context, that change all the time, so we should focus on our own repositories and work on improving as-is〔?〕. [seg 907]

## Improving one-shot completion

After this we went even further: let's focus on MRs. Our next question is, what prevents our one-shot completion? For this we focus on three evals: accepted human reviews, our tests, and also behavior checks generated by an LLM judge. The main change here is that we iterate against these until we come up with a success and receive a one-shot. [seg 908–910]

So we wrap everything in a skill. The output we expect from the skill is a context MR, a patch that will improve our context over time. [seg 911]

Now let's come back to our service A. We took our skill and ran multiple experiments, training against multiple MRs. We found multiple cases where we can improve our context: we found context conflicts, and we found context improvements. [seg 912–914]

Here is one good example that we found. In the AGENT.md file we had the instruction: if you want to put in a new contract, please put it in the command folder. The agent followed exactly that, the developer raised an MR, then the reviewer opened the MR and pushed back with the comment, "Oh, this should be in the core folder, please redo it." This exact case happened three times, over three MRs. A huge amount of wasted time, only because of a context conflict. This is exactly the case we are looking for, and over time we aim to achieve our one-shots. [seg 915–918]

## Key learnings

Now, as a conclusion, I want to share the top three key learnings that we found. First, reuse an open source evaluation framework. We started to build our framework from scratch, then we found a great solution, Harbor, an open source project from the Terminal-Bench creators, made for teams building this kind of thing; it's our recommendation. Second, focus on your own repository and incremental improvements. And the last learning, the key idea I want to leave you with from my talk today: you should start to use your merged MRs to train your agent. Thank you. [seg 919–924]
