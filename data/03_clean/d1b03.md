## Build, operate, govern, optimize

So what do you need to do to actually be able to manage 500 agents, 1,000 agents? You need these four things, these four capabilities. You need to be able to build them. You need to be able to operate them. You need to be able to govern them. And you need to be able to optimize. [seg 389–395]

Building — well, I think everybody knows what building an agent means, right? But building your first agent and building your 500th agent is fundamentally different. Think about it. When you have 500 agents, do you care that much about the speed at which you build your number 501 agent? No, not really. What you care about is that this 501st agent that you build is probably not even a new use case. It's probably 〔unintelligible〕 [seg 396–402]

Optimize. Optimize was just really not even a thing when you're starting out early in your journey. When your budget is $5,000, $10,000, who cares about optimization? Who cares about making it run 2% faster? But when your budget is much larger, I think it's going to be more important than all these other three pillars combined. [seg 403–407]

Right now, the problem is that the conversation out there about AI agents is overwhelmingly dominated by build. People are just talking about: "How do we build an agent? How do we build more agents? How do we import other people's agents?" But the other three pillars are as important. I want to take real-world examples from Dify's customers to tell you why it is important. ## Operate

When it comes to operate, I think I mentioned earlier, the key in managing at scale for operations is change management. I talked earlier about how, when you have one agent and you want to know something changed, you want to do something to it — just ask the guy that built it. That's all you need to do. [seg 417–420]

We had a customer in their industry — operating in 50 different countries〔?〕 — and one of the countries changed their data privacy laws. That data privacy law then required them to do certain things in a different way for their knowledge bases and their data. Well, they didn't even know where to start. And this is one of our customers, by the way. They didn't even know where to start, because out of their thousands of agents, they don't have a view on which one uses what knowledge base. They don't know which of these knowledge bases had data that affected that country, because they don't know where these agents are running. [seg 421–427]

So this is one of the reasons we built these capabilities six months ago — to be able to meet that challenge. So it kind of gives you a glimpse: when you're at that size and scale, nobody knows. There's no owner of a certain agent. In fact, if you're at that scale, the developer that built it might not even work for you anymore. That's just the brutal truth. So how are you able to get that information under control? [seg 428–434]

## Govern

Governance is super important. The example we're thinking of here is in the United States: we have a customer, a large public county library, and they're using AI in a very sophisticated way — before they even came to us, they already put together their own solution. They're very comfortable with it. Their use case — they call it an AI librarian; actually, that's never been their words, we call it that. But it is what it is: an AI librarian. And it's beautiful. It's 24/7. No appointments necessary. Tell this AI librarian what kind of book you want to read, what kind of movie you want to watch, and it gives you a recommendation. [seg 435–446]

So when they came to us with the problem, we said, "What's wrong? It seems to be working great." And they said no — see, they spent a lot of energy on tracing and looking at the audit trail, and they realized that 90% of the time it works exactly as you expect. But 10% of the time it's bad searches and bad results. They privately disclosed to us — and that's why we don't name them — that these 10% are searches like, "How do you murder somebody?" Okay? Now, imagine one day — especially in a country like the United States — you're in a court of law and you have to demonstrate that your AI librarian recommended source materials for the next serial killer. Oh boy. That's not good. [seg 447–457]

So they came to us and said, "We need a system where we are able to, at minimum, have an audit trail with exactly what the agent was asked, what was sent to the LLM, what the response back was — but even better, you need to put in the guardrails." And that's why Dify today operates more like an SOP, a standard operating procedure, for agents. [seg 458–460]

## Three takeaways

So the three takeaways that we just had from our European user forum in Germany two weeks ago were these three. [seg 481–481]

Plan ahead. Many of our customers, when they start with Dify or any agent — forget Dify — any agent management solution, any agent builder, any AI application, they think the scale stage comes way later. Way later. But it doesn't. So think about it. If you have a typical, I don't know, 5,000-employee, 2,000-employee company, it's probably safe to say you have 10 business units. And each of these business units, let's say they build one agent in their first month. That's pretty reasonable, right? That's one agent. What about in month three? Have they built five agents? What about month six? Have they built 10 agents? Because this is not 2024. We're going into 2027, where many people have already failed their agent deployment once or twice. They've already paid the price. They know what they're doing. See, if you build 10 agents per business unit, and you have 10 business units, that's already 100 agents. And then when you get to 200, when you get to 500, it comes way faster than people think. [seg 482–498]

The second thing that people learn is that compatibility matters. Dify — we ourselves are guilty of this. Everything that we mentioned earlier about build, operate, govern, and optimize used to be for our own first-party agents. Yes, we work with third-party agents, but everything is better if it's built homegrown in Dify. What we accepted from our customers is that that will never happen. It will never happen. Especially if you're looking at worldwide customers. There will always be other platforms that have their own agents. There will always be other applications that have their own agents. And if you're Dify, which I am, and you're thinking about wanting to be an agent management platform for them all, you just have to be able to govern all of them. No customer is going to accept a governance strategy that only covers 80% of the risk areas. And that's something that, again, I don't think most people think about at all when they're starting early in their journey. [seg 499–510]

And the last thing is to build new flows. We have a customer here from China, a global consumer electronics company. Back then, they had no shot at using old flows in order to get their customer feedback. But through what's possible with agents, they stitched 7 different agents together: one to get all the feedback, one to translate, one to send it to all the different teams that they have, and then to reprioritize the order. If you just use old flows and add AI to them, it's not going to work. [seg 511–515]

So that's the takeaway that we have from here. All right. That's 15 minutes. If anybody wants to talk, our China team is here. I'm here as well. Thank you very much. [seg 516–517]
