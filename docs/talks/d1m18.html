Optimizing Organization-Wide LLM Inference Cost: Seat License, Pay-per-Token, or Provisioned, and Beyond

Do Tuan Anh · Techcombank · 2026-11-05 主会场 · 17:01–17:12 · 场次 d1m18

银行自托管开源模型是否划算有一个可计算的盈亏点：把 GPU 月租金除以组织的有效混合 token 单价，用量超过它才应从按 token 付费转向 provisioned，低于它反而亏损；他们认为这条路径加上模型路由可降本三到五成。

演讲骨架

  1. 规模化阶段 token 消耗暴涨，Techcombank 把全组织的有效混合单价（每百万 token，计缓存后）作为要压低的核心指标。
  2. 企业买 LLM 推理有订阅制、按 token 付费、自购/租用 GPU 的 provisioned 三种方式，并提出 Efficiency Point 公式：GPU 月成本除以按 token 单价，得出月 token 量的切换点。
  3. 实践路径：订阅超额先转按 token，用量超过效率点再转 provisioned（开源模型如 GLM），按 token 作为兜底；预期节省三到五成。
  4. 切换不是唯一办法，语义与语言路由（尤其针对越南语、中文的 tokenization 成本）也能显著降本。

讲者提出的框架

  • Efficiency Point（效率点）：分子是 provisioned GPU 的月成本，分母是按 token 付费的有效混合单价，得到月 token 量盈亏点，超过才切换到自托管。
  • 三种付费方式与切换顺序：订阅制（如 $200 套餐）超额后先转按 token 付费，用量超过效率点再转 provisioned，并在达到最大并发时再扩容。

非共识

  • 自托管不是一律更便宜：低于效率点反而亏损，且用户偏好最强闭源模型会推高成本，需用公式判断切换时点。
    Below this point, you actually run a deficit, because your GPU is not at an efficient consumption rate and you pay the full price of the GPU, since you are renting it.
  • 除自托管外，语义路由与语言路由能因 tokenization 对越南语、中文的低效而显著降本，值得与 provisioned 并行采用。
    To give you an example, some models are not as efficient in Vietnamese and Chinese as other models, so we pay very close attention to tokenization tax〔?〕.

信号

  • 关注的指标追踪全组织的有效混合每百万 token 成本（输入输出加权并扣除缓存），而非单一模型价格，说明关注点是规模化后的总拥有成本。
  • 市场格局 / 规律企业用户从订阅制编码工具套餐（如 Claude Code）超额转向按 token 的开源模型 API，规模更大时转向在云上租 B200 自托管开源模型，GPU 稀缺是最大障碍。
  • 市场格局 / 规律企业数据平台（如 Databricks）的 AI Gateway 承担主备路由，让 provisioned 与按 token 的同一模型无缝兜底。

The cost problem

This chart resonates with everyone. This is the adoption chart: every organization goes through three phases, the pilot phase, the adoption phase, and then scale. At the scaling phase, you start seeing massive consumption of tokens. Your AI TCO, basically your cost, is growing off the chart. seg 1957–1966

During last year, we spent a lot of time actively researching what we should do about this cost, and it all boiled down to one single metric that we have been trying to bring down. There are a lot of other good metrics, like caching, increasing the caching rate, optimizing the input-to-output ratio, but this is the metric that we spent a lot of energy and time on bringing down: the effective blended cost per million tokens. It is basically the cost of input and output of the whole organization, after the caching rate. seg 1967–1972

For most organizations, as with ourselves, we've seen this cost slightly going down, but from time to time it actually goes up quite exponentially, because of user behavior: we tend to want to use the best model out there, such as Opus. We opt to not use Sonnet or Haiku that much anymore. So that cost is going up. seg 1973–1976

Three ways to pay the bill

To give you a better perspective, of all the available methods to pay the bill, there is subscription-based, basically paying for Claude Code, Kiro from AWS, or Ollama as AI inference. There's pay-per-token, which is what most companies use nowadays. And then there's provisioning, where you own or rent the GPUs from hyperscalers such as AWS. seg 1977–1981

The efficiency point

Last year we published a research paper called "The Efficiency Point". This is to save you the time of reading a very long research paper, so this is the math behind it: the numerator is the cost of running a provisioned GPU, and the denominator is the cost per token for pay-per-token. seg 1982–1984

To give you a real example of how we apply this math, this is the biggest question nowadays: should we now host GLM 5.3? We have run a lot of experimental setups, with B200 on AWS or even H200, but B200 nowadays provides the best cost efficiency and maximum token output. If you have a chance to get your hands on B300, that would be awesome, but it's not available for most people yet. With B200, the AWS flat price is roughly $140 per hour. seg 1985–1990

Now a real example: if your company runs 67 billion tokens per month, should I be switching? On the right-hand side you see the cost of pay-per-token, where the effective blended cost is $2. To break it down, GLM 5.3 nowadays charges around $1 per million input and $5 per million output. Normally your organization's input-to-output ratio should be around 4 to 1 for general purpose, and for heavy use cases like tool calling, MCP, and RAG agents, the ratio can range from 10 to 1, to 15 to 1, to 50 to 1〔?〕. seg 1991–1997

So the left-hand side is the cost of pay-per-token at the organization level, and the right-hand side is the cost of provisioning. We will provision 2 nodes of AWS with 8 B200 GPUs each, so 16 B200 GPUs, and that will cost around $166K per month. With this number, it's very evident that you should not be switching, because you are running a 3K〔?〕 deficit for your organization. seg 1998–2002

So now the question is, when should I be switching? Now you have all the ingredients to calculate. $166K to provision the model, $2 effective cost per million tokens, so you can calculate the token point at which you should switch, which we call the efficiency point. The efficiency point is around 83 million〔?〕. What does this mean? If your organization consumes more than 83 million〔?〕 tokens per month, you should consider switching, and that is the point where you start gaining efficiency. Below this point, you actually run a deficit, because your GPU is not at an efficient consumption rate and you pay the full price of the GPU, since you are renting it. seg 2003–2010

One problem that we see in real practice is that provisioning the model is limited by concurrency, CCU. We have tried a lot of CCU setups, and for now we still strongly believe that GLM 5.3 at one node with 8 B200 is the best setup. seg 2011–2015

This is basically the whole presentation in just one slide, and the chart is quite clear. Below the efficiency point, you run a very big deficit, and above the efficiency point, you gain efficiency in cost quite dramatically. In our practice, we found that this is the path to the best cost efficiency. seg 2016–2018

It's quite a no-brainer to switch from credit-based to pay-per-token, because most of us run Claude Code with $200 plans and it's just not enough, and we run into overage. Overage cost is quite high, and just switching to pay-per-token using GLM is much more efficient. You should consider switching from pay-per-token to provisioning when you reach the efficiency point. And once you start in provisioning mode, you should consider when to scale up: if you reach maximum concurrency. seg 2019–2025

Expected savings

At the end of the day, how much money do you expect to save? We expect, and strongly believe, that 30 to 50 percent cost efficiency is quite achievable. Here is an example: if your company runs 300 billion tokens per month, with pay-per-token you are spending 500K. If you run provisioning, you should be spending 200K. That is 58 percent cost efficiency. seg 2026–2032

On top of that, we have seen cost efficiency of more than 50 percent due to the enterprise nature. This is influenced a lot by the savings plans you have, because you rent EC2 from hyperscalers such as AWS, GCP, or Azure, which come with multi-year savings plans, and then there's the enterprise discount your company has negotiated. That can influence the price quite a lot. On the other side, the operational staff cost, the cost of maintaining auto scaling, is also worth considering. seg 2033–2037

Caveats

So there are three problem statements before you consider switching from pay-per-token to provisioning. First, GPUs are actually quite scarce. For some companies it is quite hard to get your hands on GPUs, so you need to plan ahead and deal with the hyperscaler to get them. That is the biggest blocker now. seg 2038–2042

Secondly, if we set up auto scaling, we have seen cases where we need to grow from 12 GPUs to 15 GPUs due to peak-time traffic. So it's best to keep pay-per-token as a fallback rather than removing it completely. In our setup, Databricks provides Unity AI Gateway, so it is achieved at the platform level: we can set up GLM provisioned as the main model and GLM pay-per-token as the fallback. When we ran the trial, we usually saw that 10 percent of the time the pay-per-token model is invoked, and the customer does not realize any difference between provisioned and pay-per-token. seg 2043–2048

Thirdly, the numbers are rough numbers, because they are influenced a lot by the enterprise nature, such as deals with hyperscalers, enterprise discounts, and savings plans. They can change the numbers quite drastically. seg 2049–2051

Beyond provisioning

Lastly, we also strongly believe that switching from pay-per-token to provisioning is not the only way to achieve cost efficiency. We are now adopting semantic model routing and linguistic model routing as well, using the newly released OpenJev〔?〕. In our new IPS〔?〕 research paper on tokenization tax〔?〕, we found that linguistic model routing and semantic model routing can also save cost by 40 to 50 percent. To give you an example, some models are not as efficient in Vietnamese and Chinese as other models, so we pay very close attention to tokenization tax〔?〕. seg 2052–2058

Recap

That will be the end of the talk. To recap, I leave you two bullet points. The first: there is an efficiency point, for which your company can use the formula to calculate at which point you should consider switching from pay-per-token to provisioning. Secondly, we strongly believe that 30 to 50 percent cost optimization is quite achievable. Thank you again. I'm TA from Techcombank. Hope you have a wonderful evening ahead. seg 2059–2065
